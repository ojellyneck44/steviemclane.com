SLD

A complete overhaul of a small artist's visual brand identity, complete with a unified design language, a simple yet effective logo, and everything an artist could need to market themselves.

Role
Creative director, brand and cover art designer
Client
SLD (sheluvdanny), musical artist
Timeline
2025 to 2026
Deliverables
Logo, style guide, 15 single covers

I set out to create a brand system flexible enough to survive the artist's range without disappearing into it: a simple wordmark and a compact "SLD" glyph that could fit in anywhere on any cover, no matter how loud the artwork underneath got, plus a lightweight set of rules for color, type, and logo placement so every release still reads as part of the same body of work. The result is a catalog that can jump genres and moods freely while still looking unmistakably like one artist's brand.

Step 1

It starts in a sketchbook

Every part of SLD began on paper. For the logo I worked through letter combinations and weights: S, L and D set side by side, dark and light versions of a joined S and D, and the letters pushed against a checkerboard and a speaker-style arc.

The cover sketches did the same job for single releases. Bankroll played on the "ten bands" line, pairing the title with a checkerboard pattern and two cartoon figures. HOT N' COLD planned a streetwear look in red and blue with puffy white outlines, so the color story was settled before the final artwork started.

Step 2

One mark, any background

The final mark folds the S, the L and the D into a single glyph. It stays legible as a small streaming thumbnail and holds its shape on top of the busiest artwork, which mattered for an artist whose covers change style from one release to the next.

Around the mark I wrote a one-page style guide that names the look, Y2K digital naive: early-2000s web nostalgia, MS Paint and pixel art, and streetwear cutout collage. It fixes a five-color core palette and lists what to do and what to avoid, such as keeping contrast high and staying away from muted earth tones and polished glassmorphism.

Step 3

Fifteen covers, one artist

Each single gets its own cover built around the tone of the track, held together by the mark and the palette. The styles move from pixel art to collage to photography, and the brand still reads the same from one release to the next.

Pick a cover to hear the song it belongs to. The visualizer takes its colors from whichever cover is playing.
